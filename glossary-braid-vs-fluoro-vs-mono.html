Braid vs fluorocarbon vs monofilament — which line should I use?

Mono stretches and floats; fluoro sinks, is more abrasion-resistant and less visible; braid is thin and low-stretch but slippery to knot and can break on hard hits.

Monofilament is nylon, buoyant, and stretches under load, which damps the force on the hookset and the fight. Fluorocarbon is denser and sinks faster, has greater surface hardness and abrasion resistance, and its refractive index is close to water's, making it less visible. Braid has roughly a third to a quarter the diameter of mono at the same breaking strength and almost no stretch, which is why it is so sensitive — but it is slippery and hard to knot, and its minimal stretch can break on hard-hitting fish.
