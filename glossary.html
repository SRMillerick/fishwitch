Fishing conditions glossary

baromoon's report is built from conditions — water temperature, where the fish sit, light, clarity, cover. These are the plain-language explainers, and every factual claim quotes the agency or reference source it came from. Nothing here changes a ranking; it is the explanation layer.

Water temperature & seasons

  • What water temperature do bass prefer?
    Largemouth bass seek roughly 77–80°F in summer, spawn around 60–75°F, and slow sharply below 50°F.
  • When do bass spawn, and where do they stage?
    Largemouth bass stage as the water nears about 55°F and spawn in the 60–75°F range; the male then guards the nest for 10–14 days.
  • How do you catch bass in cold water?
    Below about 50°F bass feed little and sit deep; stay in contact with the bottom and move slowly.

Weather & pressure

  • How does barometric pressure affect fishing?
    Fish feed as pressure changes: high pressure tends to produce feeding, low pressure sends fish to cover ahead of a front, and the best bite often comes just before the weather turns and again a few days after the pressure rises.

Where the fish are

  • Where do bass go in summer?
    They move to cooler, shadier water near the bottom and hold in the 77–80°F band; below the stratification level there is no oxygen, so fish stay above it.
  • What is lake stratification, and what does turnover do to the fishing?
    In summer many lakes split into layers and the water below the stratification level loses oxygen; fall cooling mixes them again and sends bass deep.

Feeding & light

  • What time of day do bass feed best?
    Bass are most active at dawn and dusk; low light also changes how they see a lure.

Color & clarity

  • What color lure should I use in stained or clear water?
    Stained water or low light: contrast beats color (dark, chartreuse, black-and-blue). Clear water, bright light, near the surface: natural colors read true. Deep: reds disappear first, blue-green persists.

Rig choice

  • When should I use a Texas rig vs a Carolina rig?
    Texas rig for shallow cover and vegetation; Carolina rig as a deep-water search lure.

Line

  • Braid vs fluorocarbon vs monofilament — which line should I use?
    Mono stretches and floats; fluoro sinks, is more abrasion-resistant and less visible; braid is thin and low-stretch but slippery to knot and can break on hard hits.

Terminal tackle

  • Do you set the hook with a circle hook?
    No — let the fish load the rod and come tight. Circle hooks are shaped to rotate into the corner of the mouth and hook the fish themselves.
