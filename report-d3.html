Line & knots for these rigs

  • Fluorocarbon (PVDF) — sinks, is less visible (refractive index near water), and has less stretch/memory for direct contact Knots quoted from the cited guides — facts, not folklore.
  • Snell knot (line-to-hook-shank) — The snell knot is a hitch knot used in angling to attach a fishing line to the shank (instead of the eye) of a fishing hook. (Wikipedia — Snell knot)
  • Palomar knot (line-to-terminal) — The Palomar knot is a knot that is used for securing a fishing line to a fishing lure, hook, or swivel. It is strong and easy to tie and with practice can even be tied in the dark. (Wikipedia — Palomar knot)
  • Uni knot (line-to-terminal, line-to-line, reel-spool) — The uni knot is a multi-purpose fishing knot used in angling that can be used for attaching the fishing line to the spool of a reel, for joining main line to leader/backing lines, and for attaching lures, swivels and snaps. (Wikipedia — Uni knot) Practice: always wet knots before tightening, regardless of line material; fluoro under 10 lb: 10 wraps when tying the snell or uni.
